03 — The 3% Rule

"

Good artists copy,
great artists steal.

— Picasso (often attributed)

Virgil Abloh's interpretation

The late designer of Off-White built his creative philosophy on what he called the "3% rule" — you only need to change 3% of something to make it yours. Not because 97% of copying is acceptable, but because that 3% is where all the meaning lives. It's where intent, context, and voice enter.

04 — The Test

Ask yourself three questions

Click each to expand

01

Did you transform it?

Transformation means the work changes form, context, or meaning. A photograph of a painting is copying. A painting that reframes the photograph's subject is transformation. Ask: did I add a new layer of meaning?

›
02

Is your voice in it?

Inspiration carries the maker's fingerprint. If you removed the reference, would the work still feel like you? Copying disappears into its source. Inspired work stands apart from it.

›
03

Could you credit the source openly?

If citing your reference would embarrass you, that's information. Genuine inspiration wears its lineage proudly. "I was inspired by Rei Kawakubo's use of negative space" is a strength, not a confession.
