# Copy audit, September 2026

Every English sentence a visitor can read on chaiyakatkwao.com, checked on 25 September 2026 against the humanizer checklist (Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"): Home, About, the four Commercial case studies, Gallery, CV, the chat bot's script, the page descriptions that show in Google and share cards, and /systems.

## Verdict

The site mostly reads like a person. Three things stand out.

1. One sentence reads like a model, and it appears in five places (item 1).
2. The four case-study briefs on Commercial carry the most tells: long dashes, groups of three, and sayings that sound deep (items 7 to 10).
3. The CV bullets were abstract. Rewritten in this branch, with the print sheet fixed for resume parsers (see "CV" below).

Labels, buttons, page descriptions, the Services section and /systems pass.

Nothing below has been applied to the site except the CV and the chat script's experience block. Each item gives the current line, what gives it away, a proposed line, and the tradeoff. CK decides.

## Site copy

### 1. The one sentence, in five places

Current: "Chaiya is a Bangkok-based Creative Producer working at the intersection of art direction and technical execution."

Where: About lead, the About block on Home, the CV lead, the About page description, the chat script.

Why it reads as AI: "working at the intersection of X and Y" is a stock phrase. It names two nouns and says nothing about what he does with them. "Technical execution" is an abstraction you cannot picture.

Proposed (About): "Chaiya is a Creative Producer in Bangkok. He art-directs the work and builds the technical side that carries it: lighting, cameras, sound and the studio's own software."

Proposed (Home and CV, no name): "Creative Producer in Bangkok. Art direction on one side; lighting, cameras, sound and the studio's own software on the other."

Tradeoff: two sentences instead of one, and the tidy symmetry of "art direction and technical execution" goes. In return the reader gets four things they can picture. On the CV, "Creative Producer in Bangkok." as the first line after the name is also exactly what a resume parser wants to see as the headline.

### 2. About, paragraph two

Current: "Raised between the rhythms of rural Udon Thani and the density of Bangkok, he came to image-making through lived experience — not theory. He studied Photography at Chiang Mai University, where his practice culminated in Woven Memories (2025), a photographic project tracing identity, memory, and everyday life."

Why: "lived experience — not theory" argues with a claim nobody made. The dash. "Rhythms of rural" is brochure language. "Culminated in" inflates a degree project.

Proposed: "He grew up between rural Udon Thani and Bangkok, and was making pictures before he studied them. At Chiang Mai University the degree ended in Woven Memories (2025), a photographic project about identity, memory and everyday life."

Tradeoff: "rhythms" and "density" were doing mood work. If CK wants the mood back, keep one of the two, not the pairing.

### 3. About, paragraph three

Current: "Now at Ad The Top Agency, he develops multi-camera productions and live content systems — translating visual ideas into structured workflows that hold up at scale."

Why: the dash, the "-ing" tail bolted on, and "hold up at scale", which cannot be checked.

Proposed: "At Ad The Top Agency he runs multi-camera productions and live commerce sessions for several brands, and writes the procedures and software the studio runs them on."

Tradeoff: none of substance. It says what the CV and /systems already say.

### 4. About, the highlighted closing line

Current: "He is interested in one thing: making creative work that functions as well as it looks."

Why: "interested in one thing:" is a drum roll before the point. The point itself is good.

Proposed: "The work has to function as well as it looks." Keep it highlighted.

Tradeoff: the drum roll is also what makes it a tagline. A person can write this line on purpose; the rule fires because a model writes it by default. If CK reads it aloud and hears himself, keep it.

### 5. About, the Systems paragraph

Passes. "Reads his calendar, mail, weather and the news before he does" is the most human line on the page.

### 6. Services and How it works

Pass. "No full edits." is a scope line, not a dramatic fragment. Keep.

### 7. Commercial, Knack Factory

Current, paragraph two: "Industrial precision meets creative expression — a new generation of Thai fashion making its first public turn."

Why: "X meets Y" is an aphorism, then a dash, then a fragment.

Proposed: "It was the first public turn for a new generation of Thai fashion graduates."

Paragraph one has one dash. Replace it with a colon: "The runway is reimagined as an assembly line: each designer's "knack," their specialised skill and distinct identity, processed as a working part of a larger machine that refines raw ideas into wearable art."

Note: if that paragraph is the show's own concept text, keep it word for word and credit it. Quotes are exempt.

### 8. Commercial, BAKAO

Current: "The brief was to shoot a lookbook that felt unhurried — clothes worn by real people in real light, without the urgency of trend-driven editorial. The approach was sparse: minimal location scouting, a tight colour palette pulled from the garments themselves, and long pauses between frames. The result is a set of images that sit closer to portraiture than fashion photography."

Why: the dash; "trend-driven"; a group of three set up by "The approach was sparse:".

Proposed: "The brief was a lookbook that felt unhurried: clothes on real people in real light, with none of the rush of a trend editorial. The shoot took little scouting, a palette pulled from the garments themselves, and long pauses between frames. The pictures came out closer to portraiture than fashion photography."

Tradeoff: still three items in the middle sentence, because there were three real choices. It now reads as a list of choices rather than a rhythm.

### 9. Commercial, Khun Chang Khian

Current: "Khun Chang Khian is a village outside Chiang Mai where craft, community, and landscape are inseparable. The assignment began as a portrait series but expanded as the place itself became the subject — small fires burning at the edge of rice fields, hands at work, light that moves differently up north." Then: "The photographs resist compression into a single theme. They are about proximity and patience, and the particular quality of time that only exists when you stay somewhere long enough to stop being a visitor."

Why: "craft, community, and landscape are inseparable" is three abstract nouns and a big word. The dash. "Resist compression into a single theme" is a saying dressed as depth.

Proposed: "Khun Chang Khian is a village outside Chiang Mai. The assignment began as a portrait series and widened as the place took over: small fires at the edge of the rice fields, hands at work, a light that moves differently up north." Then: "There is no single theme. The pictures are about staying somewhere long enough to stop being a visitor."

Tradeoff: loses "proximity and patience". The fires, hands and light stay because they are things he saw, not a rhythm.

### 10. Commercial, Podcast & Studio

Current: "Across an ongoing slate of shows, the role spans pre-production to post — guest coordination, signal routing, multi-camera direction, and final delivery. The work is less about any single episode and more about building a machine that runs consistently, week after week."

Why: the dash; "less about X and more about Y".

Proposed: "Across an ongoing slate of shows the role runs from pre-production to post: guest coordination, signal routing, multi-camera direction and final delivery. The job was to build a machine that runs the same way week after week."

Paragraph one passes. "The studio was designed to disappear." is a good first line. Keep it.

## CV

### What resume parsers saw before this branch

The live /cv page was printed to PDF on 25 September 2026 with headless Chrome, then read with three PDF text readers (pdftotext in its two modes, and pypdf). These are the same kind of readers an applicant tracking system runs before a human sees anything.

- The name came out as "Chaiya /" and "Katkwao." on two lines. None of the three readers found a name.
- Every bullet point came out at the end of the file, after the footer, cut off from its job. The dash in front of each bullet was drawn with position: absolute, and positioned text is painted after all the normal text, so the PDF stores it last.
- One reader spaced the section headings out letter by letter: E X P E R I E N C E. The headings are what a parser keys on. Cause: the 0.28em tracking.
- The two-column layout itself was fine. Each reader took the left column whole, then the right.

### Fixed in this branch

- On paper the name is one plain line, "Chaiya Katkwao". On screen it keeps the slash and the break.
- Bullets use a hanging indent instead of a positioned dash, on screen and on paper. Same look, and the text now stays in order.
- The tracked headings relax to 0.1em on paper only.
- Verified after the change: still one A4 page, and all three readers give the name on one line, the bullets under their jobs, and the headings whole.

### Bullets rewritten

The Ad The Top bullets said things like "Translates brand briefs into structured visual execution", which is the sentence a model writes when it has no facts. They were rewritten with facts the site already states, mostly from /systems: what he wrote, what he built, how many items. Nothing was added that the site does not say. The chat script's experience block carries the same wording.

### Not changed, CK's call

- "Co-ordinator". A recruiter searching "Coordinator" will not match "Co-ordinator". It is the real title at Ad The Top, so it stays. Worth knowing.
- Tool names. The Skills list names no editing, grading, switching or streaming software. Parsers and recruiters search by product name. Not added, because the site does not say which ones he uses. This is the single biggest keyword gap on the CV.
- Date ranges use the long dash ("2026 — Present"). All three readers handled it. Older parsers prefer "2026 - Present". Left to match the rest of the site.
- Two columns. Fine in testing. A single column is the safest shape for very old parsers, but the one-page design is worth more.

### On AI-writing detectors

Some recruiters also run CVs through AI-writing detectors. Those tools score the same habits the humanizer flags: abstract nouns, every sentence the same length, no numbers, no names. The new bullets have numbers, product names and uneven lengths. No detector can be promised a pass. The reliable defence is to write only what happened, in the words you would say out loud.
